Mobile app · Flutter · Gemini AI

EVERYFIT

OVERVIEW

EveryFit is a clothing shopping app that personalizes results to your body type. You pick your fit once during onboarding (petite, regular or tall), and every search filters to match. Product pages include an AI-generated summary of real customer reviews focused on quality, comfort and sizing.

PROCESS DOCUMENTATION

How I thought about it

Before writing any code, I mapped every page of the app and what each one needed to show, from opening the app to finding something worth buying. The core idea was simple: someone who is petite shouldn’t have to scroll past tall-fit results, so the app should handle that filtering quietly in the background instead of making users set it up on every search. I picked the ASOS API on RapidAPI for its large, well-organized catalog. Then I found out early that two ASOS APIs from the same source didn’t share product IDs: one had the catalog and the other had the reviews. Reading the JSON side by side made that obvious, so I chose to build around the review API, because review data was the foundation for the AI feature I cared about most.

How I built it

The API has no sizing field, so I scanned product descriptions for “petite” or “tall” and treated items with neither as regular sizing, which mirrors how ASOS labels its own inventory. A short onboarding flow saves the user’s fit with SharedPreferences, so it fills in the filter automatically on every visit, and a client-side price slider and result limit give extra control. On the product page, I fetch the reviews and pass them into a structured Gemini prompt: exactly two sentences, no bullets or bold, ignore N/A fields. That way the output renders cleanly in a plain Text widget. I chose Gemini 1.5 Flash and kept the output short to control token costs. The page shows a loading state while the summary arrives, falls back to a friendly message if anything fails, and checks mounted before each state update to avoid crashes. In the cart, you swipe one way to open the item on ASOS and the other way to remove it.

What I learned

The hardest parts weren’t the UI. They were the places where systems meet: two APIs that didn’t share data, an AI model that needed careful prompting to produce usable output, and async state in Flutter that required really understanding page lifecycles. I used AI and Stack Overflow to learn the concepts, not to write the code for me. In a second version I’d move API keys out of the client and behind a backend proxy, keep the cart between sessions, switch to a proper state management approach, and power fit recommendations with real size-chart measurements instead of keyword matching.
